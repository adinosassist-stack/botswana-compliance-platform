# v50 — PulaGo competitive differentiation & 3-pass adversarial review

## Competitor reviewed
PulaGo, operated by On-Basic (Pty) Ltd in Botswana.

Observed public positioning as at 31 August 2026:
- company incorporation and name reservation
- CIPA forms / constitution preparation
- BURS registration
- trade / industrial licence wizard
- compliance calendar and reminders
- document vault
- PPRA readiness
- concierge filing through vetted professional partners
- company secretary
- bank-account preparation
- partner-firm dashboard/channel
- startup bundle and transparent filing/service fees
- self-service plans described as coming soon

## Pass 1 — Commercial overlap attack

### Finding
A generic “Botswana SME compliance app” overlaps too heavily with PulaGo.
Direct overlap exists in CIPA, BURS, licences, reminders, vaults, tenders, partner professionals and business setup.

### Failure scenario
If both products are described as “tell us about your business and we keep you compliant,” a customer will choose the product with the clearer immediate transaction: register the company, get the TIN/licence and let someone file it.

### Fix
Do not lead with incorporation or filing.
Primary ICP becomes an operating SME with:
- 5–50 employees or meaningful employment exposure
- premises or operating licences
- PAYE/VAT/recurring obligations
- tenders or corporate-customer documentation demands
- manufacturing / regulated activities
- multi-branch operations
- inspection/audit exposure

Acquisition message:
“Protect the business after it starts trading.”

## Pass 2 — Product moat attack

### Finding
Calendar + vault + licence wizard + forms are not defensible moats. PulaGo already offers them publicly and can extend self-service.

### Durable differentiation to build around
1. Employer Shield
   - employee files
   - fixed-term / probation controls
   - leave evidence
   - warnings / discipline / grievances
   - hearing records
   - high-risk termination/retrenchment review gates
2. Regulatory Intelligence
   - approved-source registry
   - rule versioning
   - authoritative-source conflict blocking
   - company-specific rule impact
3. Compliance Obligation Engine
   - published rule → applicability → obligation → due date → escalation
4. Evidence Chain
   - hash
   - quarantine
   - review
   - obligation linkage
   - audit trail
5. Inspection Readiness
   - missing proof
   - escalations
   - readiness packs
6. Tender Control
   - tender-specific requirements and evidence readiness, not merely PPRA registration
7. Compliance Passport
   - scoped, expiring, revocable verification for banks / customers / counterparties
8. Partner Governance
   - explicit client consent and scoped cross-tenant access

### Fix
Treat setup/filing as an integration or professional-service edge, not the product identity.

## Pass 3 — Positioning, pricing and execution attack

### Risk: underpricing
P99/P249/P499 is good for adoption but can make a high-trust risk-control product look like a reminder app.

### Fix
Keep launch prices for now, but frame plans by control maturity:
- Monitor — P99
- Protect — P249
- Control — P499
Backend plan IDs remain starter/business/pro to avoid migration risk.

Do not publicly promise pricing is permanent.
Reassess after 25–50 paying businesses using:
- support minutes per tenant
- professional-review frequency
- notification/provider cost
- evidence storage
- AI usage
- partner revenue
- churn by plan

### Risk: competitor follows features
A competitor can copy UI features.

### Fix
Moat must be data/process depth:
- Botswana rule graph
- approved source provenance
- company-specific applicability history
- evidence history
- employment process history
- regulatory-impact history
- inspection packs
- verified Compliance Passport relationships
- accountant / HR / legal partner workflow data

### Risk: customer confusion with government filing services
Keep explicit boundary:
- software is not CIPA/BURS/PPRA
- professional filing is separately provided where offered
- software outputs are compliance-management outputs, not legal opinions

## Final strategic position

PulaGo:
“Start the company, prepare the forms, file the paperwork and keep basic compliance moving.”

Thebe Desk:
“Once the business is operating, continuously detect regulatory and employment risk, convert rules into controlled actions, prove compliance with evidence, and stay ready for inspections, disputes, tenders and counterparties.”

## What not to build as the core
- logo design
- letterhead
- bank-account opening concierge
- incorporation as the hero product
- generic form filling
- generic document vault
- generic deadline reminders

## What to prioritize next
1. Employer Shield 2.0 depth
2. inspection / enforcement simulation
3. business-risk event engine
4. professional-review marketplace quality controls
5. verifiable Compliance Passport adoption
6. partner portfolio risk dashboard
7. source-linked regulatory alerting
8. live integrations with authorized regulator/provider APIs where possible
